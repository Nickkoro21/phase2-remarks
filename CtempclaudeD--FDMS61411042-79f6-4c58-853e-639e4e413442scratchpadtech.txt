==== 1.4.2 Flying Safety
SUMMARY: "Once airborne, safety assumes a more dynamic character; a converging A/C may unexpectedly appear or a malfunction can cause distractions during a critical phase of flight. Safe operation requires an aggressive disposition towards gaining and maintaining situational awareness. Situational awareness is an aircrew member's continuous perception of self and A/C in relation to the dynamic environment of flight, threats, and mission, and the ability to forecast, then execute, tasks based upon that perception."
ERRORS: [
 "Allowing a malfunction or an unexpected converging A/C to cause distraction during a critical phase of flight.",
 "Failure to establish an emergency recovery plan before an emergency occurs."
]

==== 1.6 Checklist Discipline
SUMMARY: "GR 1T-6A-11CL, The Pilot's Pocket Checklist, is a condensed version of GR 1T-6A-11. The omission of a checklist item could lead to a dangerous situation; therefore positively confirm completion of all checklists regardless of how they are accomplished (for example, memory aid, mnemonic, approved unit-developed checklist or flight crew checklist). One technique to ensure accomplishment of every step is to execute a few items from memory then reference the checklist page to verify completion. Another technique is to complete the entire checklist and then refer back to the checklist to verify completion."
ERRORS: [
 "Omission of a checklist item — could lead to a dangerous situation.",
 "Failure to positively confirm completion of all checklists.",
 "Treating a '(BOTH)' item as complete without receiving a proper response from the PNF.",
 "Starting a new checklist before completing the previous one.",
 "Giving lip service to performing a checklist when using a standardized phrase or mnemonic instead of actually performing the checklist item or required check."
]
CORR: [
 "Execute a few items from memory then reference the checklist page to verify completion; or complete the entire checklist and then refer back to it to verify completion.",
 "If interrupted, restart at the first step of the checklist or two to three steps prior to the missed or interrupted step."
]
==== 1.8 Cockpit/Crew Resource Management (CRM)
SUMMARY: "The CRM program is designed to develop aircrew skills in recognizing and responding to the conditions that lead to aircrew error. While flying, CRM is the effective use of all available resources to safely and efficiently accomplish mission objectives. CRM focuses aircrew members on agencies, procedures, and resources available to enable mission success; locally developed CRM checklists describe behaviours to reinforce and others to avoid."
ERRORS: [
 "Failure to recognize and respond to the conditions that lead to aircrew error.",
 "Not using all available resources to safely and efficiently accomplish mission objectives.",
 "Failure to recognize each other's limitations and plan or act accordingly (wingman consideration).",
 "Failure to establish priorities or alter a course of action based on new information.",
 "Non-compliance with standard operating procedures and checklist discipline.",
 "Attempting to completely eliminate risk rather than matching risk to the mission."
]
CORR: []
==== 1.9 Operational Risk Management (ORM)
SUMMARY: "Flying is inherently risky. ORM is the process used to identify and reduce the risks of flying to an acceptable level. ORM trades off training benefit versus risk. Risk can often be minimized by mission changes that do not negatively impact training."
ERRORS: [
 "Failure to identify and reduce the risks of flying to an acceptable level.",
 "Not considering environmental conditions, mission profile, or pilot factors in the risk assessment."
]
CORR: [
 "Minimize risk by mission changes that do not negatively impact training."
]

==== 1.14 Tandem Seat Challenges
SUMMARY: "Limited visibility of the crewmember in the other cockpit makes it difficult to judge intentions, anticipate actions, and verify A/C systems configuration. Verbal communication must compensate for the lack of inter-cockpit visibility. Most functions are controllable from either cockpit; however some items can only be manipulated or directly checked in the front cockpit."
ERRORS: [
 "Failing to compensate with verbal communication for the lack of inter-cockpit visibility — misjudging intentions, failing to anticipate actions, and failing to verify A/C systems configuration.",
 "Not knowing which systems are controlled only from the FCP or only from the RCP.",
 "Altimeter settings not coordinated (they are set independently in each cockpit)."
]
CORR: [
 "Consider FCP pilot landing if RCP visibility is severely compromised."
]
==== 1.15 Transfer of A/C and Systems Control
SUMMARY: "Only one pilot, at a time, can fly the A/C. It is vital for flight safety to clearly establish who is the PF and who is the PNF, as fatal accidents have occurred when two pilots attempted to fly the A/C simultaneously. The PF is responsible for checklist completion and systems operation; however, the PF may task the PNF to operate systems. Using the exact words is critical to establish proper habit patterns that enhance swift, unambiguous transfer of A/C control."
ERRORS: [
 "Two pilots attempting to fly the A/C simultaneously — fatal accidents have occurred.",
 "Use of other words such as 'it' or 'jet' in lieu of the term 'A/C' — they can be misunderstood, misheard, and create confusion.",
 "Relinquishing control before the other pilot has positively assumed control (shaken the control stick).",
 "Hesitating to relinquish control when directed by the IP.",
 "Inadvertent inputs caused by the 'last use, last in control' setup of T-6 systems when systems use is not coordinated (canopy, RMU, GPS, EFIS)."
]
CORR: [
 "Immediately query the other crewmember in case of confusion.",
 "Use the exact words to establish proper habit patterns for swift, unambiguous transfer of A/C control."
]
==== 7.4 TASK MANAGEMENT
SUMMARY: "Unlike contact flying, the horizon is not used to determine pitch and bank; instrument flight uses the EADI for attitude information. As visual cues become less prominent, a greater percentage of attention must be focused on the EADI. Time-consuming tasks (approach plate review, chart manipulation, NAVAID setup, GPS programming) are accomplished as much as the workload allows, with A/C basic control always the first priority. Effectively trimming the A/C significantly reduces workload and is the key to successfully accomplishing other subtasks."
ERRORS: [
 "Allowing complex tasks that divert attention from basic instrument flight to be attempted as a single block instead of being broken down into subtasks.",
 "Failing to trim effectively, which increases the workload of controlling the A/C and degrades accomplishment of other subtasks.",
 "Failing to anticipate required actions (frequencies, NAVAIDs not pre-set in the RMU standby/queue)."
]
CORR: [
 "Break the complex task into subtasks; complete one subtask then return full attention to the instruments.",
 "Alternate attention between subtasks and the instruments until the full task is complete."
]
==== 7.5 COCKPIT ORGANIZATION
SUMMARY: "An organized approach to cockpit setup is essential, due to the additional publications (charts and approach plates) required on most instrument sorties. Cockpit organization during mission planning is of major importance when it gets busy in the A/C. In general, strive to find ways to get ahead and prepare for upcoming tasks."
ERRORS: [
 "Publications not prefolded / not stored in order of use, causing disorganized cockpit when workload is high.",
 "Not writing down radio frequencies as they are assigned.",
 "Not using the RMU standby frequency function to anticipate radio changes."
]
